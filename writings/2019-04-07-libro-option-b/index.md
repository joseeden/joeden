# Libro: Option B

*Option B*, by Sheryl Sandberg and Adam Grant

Book 22 of 100

“Life can only be understood looking backward, but it’s meant to be lived by moving forward.”

![alt text](image.png)

The loss of a loved one is life-altering.

And this book shows how tragedy doesn’t end with the person who leaves. In many ways, the weight of it continues with the people who remain.

Grief changes routines.

Relationships.

Plans.

Even the way you imagine the future.

A few ideas from this one really stayed with me:

1. **Couples grow through dependence, but individuality still matters.**

It’s natural to rely on each other.

That’s part of being partners.

But I think there’s also value in making sure that both people continue to grow as individuals, with their own confidence, skills, relationships, and ability to make decisions.

Being together shouldn’t mean becoming incapable of standing on your own.

----------------------------------------------

2. **Help each other grow without solving everything for each other.**

This one can sound a little harsh at first.

But I don’t think the idea is to watch your partner struggle from a distance.

It’s more about knowing when to help and when to give them enough space to figure things out themselves.

Sometimes support means stepping in.

Sometimes support means saying:

**I know you can handle this, and I’m right here if you need me.**

Maybe that’s how two people grow individually while still growing together.

----------------------------------------------

3. **Prepare for the things nobody wants to think about.**

Insurance isn’t exactly the most exciting thing to talk about.

But one practical lesson from a book about loss is that tragedy doesn’t care whether your finances are ready.

The point of insurance, at its simplest, is transferring some of that financial risk so the people you leave behind don’t have to carry every burden on top of everything else they’re already dealing with.

You won’t be around to enjoy the payout anyway.

The people you care about will.

That alone is probably worth thinking about.

----------------------------------------------

Overall, *Option B* is really about what happens when **Option A is no longer available**.

You don’t necessarily get over something.

Sometimes you simply learn how to live around the space it left behind.
