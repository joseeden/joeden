# Libro: The Smartest Kids in the World

*The Smartest Kids in the World*, by Amanda Ripley
Book 25 of 100

“It was relentless and excessive, yes, but it also felt more honest.

![alt text](image.png)

Kids in hamster-wheel countries knew what it felt like to grapple with complex ideas and think outside their comfort zone. They understood the value of persistence. They knew what it felt like to fail, work harder, and do better. They were prepared for the modern world.”

For educators and teachers, the question becomes: **How do you provide a world-class education?**

And for parents: **How do you find one?**

But I guess my biggest takeaway from this book is much simpler:

**Education doesn’t start inside classrooms.
Education starts at home.**

Schools obviously matter.

Teachers matter.

Curriculum, resources, expectations, and the environment students grow up in all matter too.

But long before a child sits inside a classroom, there are already lessons being taught at home.

How failure is handled.

How curiosity is encouraged.

Whether questions are welcomed.

Whether effort matters more than simply getting the right answer.

And maybe that foundation shapes how a student eventually approaches learning everywhere else.

I wouldn’t call this a definitive guide for parents or educators.

But it does give an interesting glimpse into how students from different parts of the world are challenged, how they fail, how they recover, and what their education systems expect from them.

Different countries.

Different classrooms.

Different approaches.

But the same basic question: **How do we help people learn how to learn?**
