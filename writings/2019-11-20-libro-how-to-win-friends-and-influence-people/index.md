# Libro: How to Win Friends and Influence People

*How to Win Friends and Influence People*, by Dale Carnegie
Book 41 of 100

![alt text](image.png)

Here’s the uncomfortable part about a lot of self-help books that promise to help people become more persuasive, more confident, or better at winning people over:

Sometimes the line between **influencing people** and **using people** can get pretty thin.

That was probably my biggest thought while going through this one.

There are definitely a lot of useful ideas between the covers, especially around listening, showing genuine interest, avoiding unnecessary arguments, and understanding how people want to be treated.

But a lot of the examples also felt heavily influenced by sales, negotiation, and the idea of getting people to say yes.

And that’s where it gets interesting.

The same skill can be used to build better relationships or simply to get something from someone.

I guess the difference comes down to intent.

Are you trying to understand people better?

Or are you just trying to figure out which buttons to press?

Still, it’s hard to ignore how influential this book has been.

A lot of modern books on sales, communication, persuasion, and leadership seem to trace at least some of their ideas back to Carnegie.

So while I didn’t agree with everything, I can still see why this one became a classic.

Maybe not a perfect guide to human relationships.

But definitely one of the foundations of modern books about influence.
